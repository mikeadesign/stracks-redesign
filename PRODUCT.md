# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Men in Algonquin, IL and the Fox Valley area — ages roughly 20–65+. They want a barber they can trust, not a brand experience. They find the shop by word of mouth, a Google search, or the Facebook page. When they land on the site, the job is simple: confirm the shop is real, credible, and what they're looking for — then call. Secondary audience: locals shopping for a gift certificate.

## Product Purpose

A single-page marketing site for Strack's Barbershop, a classic old-school barbershop on South Main Street in Algonquin, IL. The site's one job is to establish authenticity and drive a phone call. It is not a booking platform, not a portfolio, and not a corporate presence — it's a digital version of the hand-painted window sign: this shop exists, it has character, it takes pride in the craft.

Business constraints: cash only (no payment flows), no online booking (pen-and-paper scheduling), no Rick memorial page.

## Brand Personality

Rugged · Timeless · No-nonsense

Old-school retro barbershop — the kind that's been there for decades and earns its reputation one cut at a time. Awards on the wall. Original leather chairs. A barber who listens. Pride in craft, not in trend.

Voice is direct, confident, and unhurried. Never precious, never ironic, never corporate. The copy already nails this; the visual system should match it.

## References

- **Vintage Americana / old print**: Mid-century barber trade cards, barbershop window signage, Popular Mechanics era graphic design. Grid discipline, ink-on-paper contrast, typography with weight and intention.
- **Abelson & Queen** (abelsonqueen.com): Refined heritage menswear — restrained, high-craft, typographically-led. The sophistication of old institutions.
- **Gentlemen Barber Clubs** (gentlemen-barberclubs.de): Dark, masculine, structured treatment. Barbershop as serious craft space, not lifestyle accessory.
- **Hagis Barbering** (hagisbarbering.com): Motion design reference — atmospheric and intentional. A ceiling, not a floor; pull back to the level the content earns.
- **Current production site** (stracksbarbershop.com): Baseline for content, voice, and local identity.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate salon chains** (Great Clips, Sport Clips, Regis): Slick, generic, soulless. The site should feel nothing like a franchise.
- **Trendy hipster barbershops**: Over-designed, Instagram-bait, all-black-with-neon. Trying too hard. Strack's doesn't need to perform authenticity — it has it.
- **Generic local business templates**: Stock photography, booking widgets, WordPress themes with contact forms. The site should feel handcrafted, not assembled.
- **SaaS / startup landing pages**: Gradient text, hero metrics, scroll-triggered marketing noise. Wrong register entirely.

## Design Principles

1. **Authenticity over polish** — every design choice should feel earned, not manufactured. Imperfect materials (aged textures, ink-on-paper contrast) beat smooth digital defaults.
2. **Typography IS the identity** — at this scale, layout and type carry the brand more than imagery. The display fonts aren't decorative; they're the shop's voice.
3. **The UX never lies** — the site reflects the actual shop experience: no booking, no subscription prompts, no upsell flows. Anything that implies features that don't exist is wrong.
4. **Old print discipline** — hierarchy and composition draw from trade cards and vintage signage. Every element earns its place; nothing is decorative noise.
5. **Motion serves atmosphere, not mechanics** — animation adds period-appropriate mood (a shimmer, a slow reveal) without becoming a feature itself. If the motion calls attention to itself, dial it back.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. The audience skews older (regulars in their 50s–70s), so body text contrast and legibility matter more than average. Default to darker body text on light sections rather than relying on muted grays. Reduced motion must be respected — all animations need a crossfade or instant fallback.
